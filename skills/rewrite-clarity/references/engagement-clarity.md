# Engagement Clarity

**Origin:** leadership-communications practice — internal announcements, event invites and broadcast updates (Grand Prix recap, fireside-chat invite, weekly updates).

Word- and message-level rules for **announcements and invites** — short, broadcast-style docs whose job is not to inform but to get attention and drive one action. Complements `writing-communication.md` (sentences) and `presentation-clarity.md` (slides). Judgement about *whether the event/announcement is worth attending* belongs elsewhere; this file covers whether the message earns the read.

## Core Principle

> An announcement competes for attention. It has one shot: **hook → takeaway → action.**

| Rule | Meaning | Test |
|------|---------|------|
| **Hook first** | Open with the question or stake that makes the reader care — before names, dates, logistics | Would a busy reader stop scrolling at line 1? |
| **Takeaway scannable** | Every header states what the reader gets, not the topic | Read only the bold headers: do you know why to come? |
| **Action explicit** | End with the single action and a reason to act now | Can the reader finish in 5 seconds: what, when, where, why now? |
| **Curiosity, not secrecy** | Tease the value of attending; never dump the content | After reading, do I know why to come but not what I'll miss by skipping? |

## Engagement Patterns

| Weak (announcement) | Strong (engaging) | Why it works |
|---------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Info-dump first line: names, org chart, logistics | **Hook question or stake** in the first line: the change, tension or "what happens when…" the reader cares about | Earns the second line |
| Topic headers: "Agenda", "Speakers", "Overview" | **Takeaway headers**: what the reader gets — "Your chance to ask Maxim directly", "3 big wins from race week" | Headers alone tell the why-attend story |
| "Join us" + date + room, full stop | **Action + excuse removal**: what to do, plus the reason to do it now (limited seats, question window, deadline) | A date without a reason gets deferred |
| "We hope to see you there" | **Forward-pointing close**: what happens at/after the event, or what the reader walks away with | Ends on the reader, not the organizer |
| All details in one dense paragraph | **Scannable blocks**: bold key facts (date, place, time), short bullets per agenda item | Skimmed in 3 seconds, still lands |
| Formal third person ("employees are invited…") | **Direct address** ("you", "your questions") | Reads like a person, not a poster |

## Set Expectations, Don't Spoil

For events (fireside chats, launches, reveals): the announcement sells **attendance**, not the content.

- **Name the theme and the tension, not the takeaways: the question being debated, who's in the room, what's at stake. Never the answers, the punchline, or the argument.
- **Frame what the audience controls**: "bring your questions", "open Q&A", "meet the speakers after" — participation beats passive listening, and it's always true.
- **One curiosity gap, honestly sized**: it's fine to imply "the discussion will go further than the title" if the prep supports it; never invent a reveal, a guest or an exclusive that isn't confirmed.
- **Logistics are expectations too**: say what the format is (30 min chat + 30 min mingle) so people can plan; a mystery format suppresses attendance.

## Honest Urgency

| Claim | When it's honest | When it's fluff |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|
| "Seats are limited" | Room capacity is real | Always-append filler |
| "Tomorrow" / "last chance" | Actually true in the calendar | Fake countdown |
| "Front row goes fast" | Specific and plausible | Generic scarcity |
| "Bring your questions for the Q&A" | Q&A is really open | Q&A is pre-screened |

If the urgency line wouldn't survive "compared to what?", cut it.

## Announcement Density

| Signal | Threshold | Fix |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Lines before the hook | > 2 | Cut greeting/formalities; hook is line 1–2 |
| Actions asked | > 1 | Pick the one that matters; move the rest to the footer |
| Time-to-action | > 15 seconds of reading | Date/place/time as bold scannable block |
| Emojis / emphasis | decoration over structure | Emojis may *label* structure (calendar pin, location pin); never replace the takeaway |
| Unconfirmed details | any (times, guests, "special surprise") | Verify or cut — a wrong invite is worse than a plain one |
