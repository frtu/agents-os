# Presentation Clarity

**Origin:** YC Startup Library — Kevin Hale (deck design), Michael Seibel (investor vs customer pitch), Janelle Tam (Series A deck); Thiel-template teardown (design do's & don'ts).

Word- and slide-level rules for pitches, product presentations and exec decks. Complements `writing-communication.md` (sentences) with rules for **titles, one-liners, numbers and slides**. Judgement about *what* the deck argues belongs to `review-product-strategy` (P1–P12) and `review-engineering-director`; this file only covers whether it reads clearly.

## Core Principle

> Before anyone can remember, they have to understand. Make it **legible**, **simple**, **obvious**.

| Rule | Meaning | Test |
|------|---------|------|
| **Legible** | Large, bold, simple font; strong contrast; key text at the top | Readable from the back row |
| **Simple** | One idea per slide / paragraph ("plex" = fold) | Can you state the idea in one sentence? |
| **Obvious** | Understood at a glance; the conclusion is written down | Flash it to a stranger: do they say the point immediately? |

## Marketing Language

Fluff the reader must filter out to find what you do. Replace with the **mechanism** (who does what, and what happens).

| Phrase | Problem | Ask for |
|--------|---------|---------|
| "platform", "solution", "ecosystem" | Says nothing about what it does | What does a user do with it, step by step? |
| "revolutionary", "reinventing", "disrupting", "game-changing" | Ambition, not mechanism | What changes for the customer, in numbers? |
| "first-ever", "unique", "only", "best-in-class", "world-class" | Unprovable superlative | Compared to what? What evidence? |
| "seamless", "effortless", "intuitive", "frictionless" | Adjective without data | How many steps / minutes vs today? |
| "AI-powered", "next-generation", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art" | Technology as value | What outcome does it produce? |
| "leverage", "synergy", "empower", "unlock", "enable" | Verb without object or result | Unlock *what*, measured how? |
| "organizes / transforms the world's X" | Slogan; only clear to people who already know you | Describe the mechanism (see Before/After) |

## Jargon by Audience

- **Customer audience:** industry jargon *builds* credibility — keep it if they use it.
- **Investor / exec / cross-functional audience:** jargon *hurts*. Replace it, or define it in five words on first use.
- Internal codenames and team names are jargon for everyone outside the team.

## One-Liner Tests

A title, subtitle or opening sentence describing what a product / initiative does must pass all three:

| Test | Weak | Strong |
|------|------|--------|
| **Concrete** | "Reinventing agriculture" | "We build and operate robot greenhouses" |
| **Layperson words** | "Behaviour change among nicotine addicts" | "Help people quit smoking" |
| **Specific to you** | "Plant-based dairy" | "We make plant-based cheese" |

## Claim Titles

Slide and section titles state the **claim**, not the topic.

| Topic title (weak) | Claim title (strong) |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| "Metrics" | "Revenue grew 3× in 6 months" |
| "Problem" | "Teams lose 2 days per release to manual sign-off" |
| "Architecture" | "One query replaces five database lookups" |

## Number Presentation

| Red flag | Why | Fix |
|----------|-----|-----|
| Undefined metric ("users", "active", "revenue") | Reader can't judge it; erodes trust | Define: monthly vs daily actives, gross vs net |
| Cumulative totals ("since launch") | Hides weak recent periods; experts notice | Per-month / per-quarter values |
| Double-axis chart | Time lost matching lines to axes | Two charts, or one metric |
| Chart with no caption | Reader must study it | Write the conclusion as the caption |
| Many numbers on one slide | Nothing retained | 2–4 hero facts; rest to appendix |
| Snapshot only | No direction | Show 4–6+ periods of trend |

## Slide Density Rules

| Signal | Threshold (guideline) | Fix |
|--------|-----------------------|-----|
| Bullets per slide | > 5 | Split or cut to the one idea |
| Words per slide body | > ~40 | Abbreviate; move detail to speaker notes / appendix |
| Full sentences as bullets | any | Abbreviate |
| Paragraph on a slide | any | "If you must read it, it's too long" |
| Screenshot / UI capture | any | Bulleted list of 3 steps |
| Complex diagram | > ~6 boxes or crossing arrows | One message; simplify or move to appendix |
| Animation / transitions / memes / stock photos | any | Remove |
| Fonts / colours | > 1 family, > 3 colours | One family (≤ 2 weights), 3 colours, slide numbers |

> **Exception:** overwhelm on purpose only when the slide's point *is* the complexity of the problem.

## Before/After Examples

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| "Google organizes the world's information." | "You type what you want to know into a box; we return an ordered list of websites, ranked by how many other sites link to them." |
| "An AI-powered platform that seamlessly empowers teams." | "Engineers ask a question in Slack and get the owning team's runbook in under 10 seconds." |
| "Medical billing is broken." | "Today, patients go through X steps, wait Y months for reimbursement, and Z% of claims are approved." |
| "We have a database of 100k entries." | "Customers verify an identity with X% accuracy in Y minutes." |
| Chart titled "Growth" | Chart captioned "Weekly actives up 20% month over month for 6 months" |

## Red Flags in Presentations

- Title slide a stranger can't paraphrase
- Topic titles instead of claim titles
- Marketing superlatives without evidence
- Undefined or cumulative numbers
- More than one idea per slide
- Screenshots used to explain what the product does
