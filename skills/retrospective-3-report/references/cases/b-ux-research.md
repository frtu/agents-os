# Case B — UX Research & Design (illustrative)

> Synthetic example of a **goal-framed** retro: the session was held to evaluate a pilot (design sprints) and decide how to continue. The trap is a report that lists stickies but never answers the question it was called for.

## Team context

- **Sub-areas:** Research (recruiting, usability testing), Product design. Works with 3 engineering squads; non-designers join workshops.
- **Group "Needs improvement" by:** Participation · Facilitation · Recruiting & testing · Delivery.
- **Recurring themes:** cross-functional participation in design work; recruiting real users; prototype-to-build handoff.
- **Key metrics to ask for:** participants per workshop (and drop-offs), days from sprint to validated prototype, users tested vs target, prototype build time vs estimate, findings adopted into the backlog.

## Retro 2026-03-06

### Raw input (as captured)

**Retrospective goal**
- Review how the design-sprint pilot fit our UX process.
- Decide how we want to continue with design sprints.

**What went well**
- Prototypes gave engineering a head start on development (+1)
- Team members receptive to the concept and eager to try it
- Design direction was a shared process
- Testing gave us valuable insights (+1)
- Felt less siloed than our normal design process
- Excited as a team to move forward with the findings

**Areas for improvement**
- Get everyone involved in the sessions — some people left early and missed important information
- Non-designers hesitant to ideate; didn't think they could contribute (+1)
- Had to explain UX concepts a lot, which slowed the session
- Prototype took much longer to build than expected (+1)
- Finding real users who matched our personas was difficult
- Hard to pull people away from their normal work to participate

**Action plan**
- Coordinate sessions with a scheduling poll for best availability — *[Owner A]*
- Engage with sales to help recruit test participants — *[Owner B]*
- Design a warm-up activity to ease non-designer hesitation — *[Owner C]*

### Review findings

1. **The goal is unanswered** — the retro was called to *decide how to continue* with design sprints. Nothing on the board says continue / change / stop. The summary must open with that decision (or state that it's still open and who decides by when).
2. **Owners named — keep this.** Every action has one person. Better than most retros.
3. **No dates, no success signals** — "coordinate sessions" by when, and how will we know it worked? e.g. "all 5 core participants attend full sprint", "≥ 5 persona-matched users recruited per test round".
4. **Highest-voted pain has no action** — "prototype took much longer than expected" (+1) is untouched. Ask: how much longer (days vs estimate)? Cause known? It's the pain most likely to decide whether sprints are affordable.
5. **Participation is one theme, three stickies** — leaving early, hard to pull people away, and scheduling are one problem: *sprint time isn't protected*. The scheduling poll treats the symptom; the cause is that managers haven't allotted the time. Consider an action at that level.
6. **Wins are feelings** — "less siloed", "receptive", "excited" are real signal for a pilot but need at least one outcome: did the head start on development shorten delivery? by how much?
7. **First-person sticky, blameless framing** — "I had to explain UX concepts a lot" → reframe as a system issue: *non-designers lack a shared primer*. Links to the warm-up action; maybe a pre-read.
8. **Positive and negative of the same thing** — "design direction was shared" vs "non-designers hesitant to ideate": both true — shared *decision*, not shared *generation*. Worth one sentence in the summary.

### What the fixed summary looks like

> **Decision: continue design sprints, one per quarter, with protected time** `[need input: confirm with team lead]`. The pilot delivered what it promised — a shared design direction and a prototype that gave engineering a head start (`[need input]`: days saved) — but it cost more than planned: the prototype overran (`[need input]`: days vs estimate) and participation leaked because sprint time wasn't protected.

> - **Top pain points** — prototype overrun; unprotected participant time; recruiting persona-matched users.
> - **Where the team wants to go** — cheaper prototypes and real cross-functional participation before scaling the practice.
