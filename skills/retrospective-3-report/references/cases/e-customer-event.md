# Case E — Customer Conference Programme (illustrative)

> Synthetic example of a **project/event retro** in a non-engineering (cross-functional) setting, using **4Ls**. The retro closes a one-off event, so "next cycle" is next year's edition — the carry-over must survive a long gap and possibly a different team.

## Team context

- **Sub-areas:** Marketing & promotion, Logistics & vendors (venue, catering, security), Programme & content (speakers, sessions), On-site tech (AV, registration).
- **Group "Needs improvement" by:** Promotion timing · Vendor management · On-site operations · Follow-up.
- **Recurring themes:** late promotion driving late sign-ups; vendor contracts signed late; no post-event follow-up.
- **Key metrics to ask for:** registrations vs attendance vs last edition, registrations by week (how back-loaded?), attendee satisfaction score, sessions rated, cost per attendee, leads/opportunities generated, on-site incidents.

## Format mapping used

| 4Ls column | Template section | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Liked** | What went well | Separate deliberate choices from luck |
| **Learned** | Needs improvement (the pain) **+** candidate actions (the lesson) | Most "learned" stickies are an action with the pain implied — recover the pain |
| **Lacked** | Needs improvement | Symptom → impact → cause |
| **Longed for** | Start (S/S/C) / wishes | Aspirations; only the voted ones become actions |
| Votes / flags | Priority | Flags often mark "must fix next time" |

## Retro 2026-09-11

### Raw input (as captured; votes / flags in brackets)

**Liked**
- Keynote speakers were really well received (2 votes, 2 flags)
- Lots of new faces from the customer community
- Weather was perfect (1 flag)
- On-site tech team sorted out issues immediately
- Attendees brought colleagues from new departments
- Great attendance — nearly **75% increase** on last year!

**Learned**
- Need to start promotion earlier — most registrations came in the last week, which caused problems planning catering and security (1)
- Set up a group channel for on-site messages (1)
- Provide clearer information on catering stand locations (1, 1 flag)
- Site managers should be more visible (uniform / badge)
- Vendor contracts must be in place at least a week before — we don't want a repeat of the facilities issue

**Lacked**
- Courteous security staff — complaints about this year's security vendor (1, 1 flag)
- Post-event evaluation and follow-up
- A wider range of sessions
- Not enough lighting on one side of the stage

**Longed for**
- Higher partner/sponsor participation, if we can show them the benefits (2, 1 flag)
- More community involvement (1, 1 flag)
- Post-event press / content
- More seating
- More food

### Review findings

1. **Headline is there — lead with it and scope it** — "+75% attendance" is the story. Is that registrations or check-ins? Against which edition? And the flip side: growth that arrived *late* strained catering, security and seating. That trade-off is the summary.
2. **One root cause behind five stickies** — late registrations → catering shortfall, security strain, not enough seating, not enough food. The cause is **promotion started late** (Learned #1). One action upstream beats five downstream.
3. **"Learned" items are already actions** — good instinct, but each needs its pain stated (what went wrong, how bad) so next year's team understands why the rule exists. "We don't want a repeat of the facilities issue" is unreadable to anyone not in the room.
4. **Luck counted as a win** — "weather was perfect" (flagged!) is external. Keep it out of What went well or mark it as luck, and turn it into a risk: *what's the bad-weather plan?*
5. **Vendor complaint, blameless framing** — "complaints about security vendor" → describe the system: brief, contract terms, and how complaints were captured (count?). The action is on vendor selection/briefing, not on individuals.
6. **Too many small items at equal weight** — lighting, uniforms, stand signage and seating are real but minor. Group as *on-site operations checklist* — one action, one owner — so the table isn't a logistics dump.
7. **Post-event follow-up is both a Lacked item and the retro's own gap** — no evaluation, no follow-up with attendees, no press. The highest-value action for the business (leads, sponsor case) and it's time-sensitive: do it now, not next year.
8. **Carry-over across a long gap** — next edition is ~12 months away and the team may change. The action table needs dated milestones (e.g. "promotion starts T-12 weeks") on a plan that outlives this team, not just owners.
9. **Sponsor participation (2 votes) needs evidence first** — "if we can show them the benefits" → the benefit numbers come from post-event evaluation (#7). Sequence the actions.

### What the fixed summary looks like

> **Attendance rose ~75% on last year** (`[need input]`: check-ins vs registrations, edition compared), driven by new community members and word of mouth — but the growth arrived in the **last week**, and late numbers strained catering, security and seating. The pattern: **promotion started too late for the size of event we now run**. Direction: plan the next edition to its new scale, and capture the value of this one before it goes cold.
>
> - **Biggest wins** — attendance growth; keynotes rated highly (`[need input]`: score); on-site tech response.
> - **Top pain points** — back-loaded registrations; vendor readiness (contracts, security conduct); no post-event follow-up.
> - **Where the team wants to go** — earlier promotion, vendors locked early, and a sponsor case built on this year's data.
