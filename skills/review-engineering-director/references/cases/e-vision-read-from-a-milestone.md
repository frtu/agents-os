# Case E — Vision judged from a milestone

- **Before:** the team demos a real but narrow first step to a senior exec. Feedback through the boss: *"wrong direction, think bigger."* The draft response is a deck with more technical detail defending the step.
- **Common approach:** defend the plan with detail, or pivot to the exec's literal suggestion.
- **Why this one diverges:** the vision was never wrong. The **artifact was wrong for the audience**. Execs read the end shape; teams read milestones. The vision had not been refreshed with this exec for months, so the increment was the only picture he had.
- **Interrogation:** *"Is the exec seeing the end shape or a milestone?"* (15). *"When did he last see the full vision?"* *"What can't it do yet, and did we say so?"* (20).
- **What surfaces:** the exec's literal ask (a technology or capability name) hides an intent, such as insight instead of aggregation. There may be a shorter path to it.
- **Distilled (what to do):** re-present the vision with a logical architecture of how the pieces connect to the company. Your boss reviews it first, then you present together. Demo at the capability level the exec cares about and ship the simpler level as an increment. Run two threads: fast visible increments, plus a blueprint so other teams' layers don't push yours down. Answer "be ambitious" with an adopted increment, not a bigger promise.
- **Lesson:** two audiences, two artifacts. Visibility decays, so refresh the vision on a cadence.
