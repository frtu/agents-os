# Case H — Partner wants to own the whole vertical

- **Before:** *"We will provide ingestion and five step-level APIs; the partner builds the experience; resourcing to be agreed."*
- **Common approach:** accept the easy groundwork to keep the peace, and split the API to reduce friction.
- **Why this one diverges:** whatever is shared can be claimed. Give only the foundation and "they build the house without us". Fine-grained APIs survive step one and lose step two, because whoever composes them owns the product.
- **Interrogation:** *"Who owns the unified thing, by name?"* (19). *"Which layer is critical and complex, and why is it not first?"* *"Did the sponsor say this in the room, or did we hear it from a peer?"*
- **What surfaces:** the partner is short-staffed and wants to offload risk. The exec mandate is to build a shared capability; the partner's preference is a floor that keeps you in place.
- **Distilled (what to do):** negotiate WHAT (layer) → WHEN → WHO contributes; never offer people first. The platform owns the HOW and the runtime; the partner owns the experience and the last mile. One coarse, extensible API with an opaque payload. Never say "we don't do X" externally. Build the critical part first: "own it right or not at all". Decide who maintains it before you build. Build from both ends.
- **Lesson:** don't let the timeline pollute the architecture. Who contributes is tactics; layers are strategy.
