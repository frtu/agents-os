# Case G — Cost crisis nobody owned

- **Before:** a written answer to finance with a page of history, several teams named, and next year's request in the same paragraph.
- **Common approach:** explain the history and share the blame.
- **Why this one diverges:** the meeting turns on one question, **who owns it now**, and history does not answer it. The engineers involved are scared and may freeze.
- **Interrogation:** *"Who owns this from today?"* (19). *"Which question is about this year and which about next year?"* *"How confident is each number?"* (4).
- **What surfaces:** the contract was signed as a project and nobody governed the drawdown. The prepaid credit was invisible. There was no breakdown per team.
- **Distilled (what to do):** split into "this year: cause and prevention" vs "next year: requirements". Own it publicly ("Yes, I will own it"). Explain the tool's value in plain terms. Set a generic baseline plus ad hoc requests with justification. Add alerts at 80/90/100% of quota so owners worry before you do. Run a short-term thread (data pulls) and a long-term one (a system people can query). Shield the team. Use a broadcast thread with finance and domain leads, copying the exec's delegate.
- **Lesson:** accountability resolves the meeting. A crisis you prepared for with data is survivable; thank whoever warned you early.
