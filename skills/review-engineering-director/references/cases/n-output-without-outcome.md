# Case N — High output, low outcome

- **Before:** a retro note: *"we delivered 40 items this quarter"*, alongside late-night messages and *"I don't feel anything lands"*.
- **Common approach:** push harder, or add process everywhere.
- **Why this one diverges:** it is a leadership problem. The leader kept planting seeds and relied on smart people, and smart people get tired.
- **Interrogation:** *"Which three items produced an outcome someone noticed?"* *"What is the first terminable milestone of each open stream?"* (17). *"Who absorbs incoming asks before they reach the team?"*
- **What surfaces:** ad hoc Kanban with no capacity buffer, firefighting mixed with big bets, and exploration with no planned return.
- **Distilled (what to do):** the leader owns it publicly. Slow down overall; keep high value plus business-as-usual. Park exploration in a backlog that actually gets revisited. Use time-boxed sprints with buffer. A two-loop org: leads scope, the team executes. Allocate depth by priority. Clean up (close catch-all channels). Give one person part-time prevention work instead of a slice of everyone. No new topics for a while.
- **Lesson:** outcome should guide, not crush. Achievement needs finish lines.
