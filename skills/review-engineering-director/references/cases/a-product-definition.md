# Case A — The boil-the-ocean vision with no wedge

- **Before:** *"We propose to build [Product] — one system to rule them all. It ingests any data, anywhere, understands it, ranks it, answers questions, and recommends. Capabilities: federation, connectors, intent understanding, multi-facet results, permissions, feedback engine. A profound impact across the whole company."*
- **Interrogation:** Three challenges, fired in order:
  1. *"What is the one decision this doc asks the reader to make, and by when?"* — a vision without an ask is a whitepaper, not a proposal. It informs; it can't be approved.
  2. *"If you could ship only one scenario for one user in the first quarter, which, and why that one?"* (heuristics 1, 7) — the capability catalog is a menu; a fundable product is a **wedge**. Everyone can list features; the judgement is in the cut.
  3. *"What is explicitly NOT in scope — and why was building even the wedge hard *before* you knew the answer?"* (13) — "any data, anywhere" hides the constraint that makes it real work; unbounded scope reads as un-costed.
- **What surfaces:** buried under the catalog is a single high-pain beachhead (one user, one job, one data source — e.g. "support staff resolving escalations without leaving their console"), a reason that scenario is the right first win (highest pain × reachable data), and one genuinely hard constraint (compliance boundaries that forbid moving the data, forcing federation rather than a central index). That constraint — not the feature list — is the actual product bet.
- **Distilled:** *"[Product] lets [one user] do [one job] without [the specific pain], starting with [the beachhead scenario]. The hard part is [the constraint that rules out the obvious approach]. In scope for the first cut: X. Explicitly not: Y, Z. The ask: [decision + spend + date]. The full capability set is the multi-year arc, sequenced behind that wedge."*
- **Lesson:** a product-definition doc is judged by its **wedge and its ask**, not the breadth of its vision. Lead with identity — who this is *for* and the one job it wins first (8) — then the single hardest constraint that made it non-trivial (13). The capability catalog is an appendix to the bet, never the bet itself; an unbounded mission with no cut and no ask is a junior artifact dressed as a strategy (1, 7).
