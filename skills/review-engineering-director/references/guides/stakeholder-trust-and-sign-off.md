# Guide — Stakeholder trust, quality bars and sign-off

**Load when:** a stakeholder distrusts readiness; a launch needs multiple sign-offs; a stakeholder dictates a solution; defining "done" or "validated". Cases: I, J, K.

## Default moves

1. **Human → process → tools.** Fix the relationship first: show *their* case working, together.
2. **Ask "for what?" before building a requested HOW** (H16). Turn the need into a contract: stakeholders add test cases, and the platform optimizes across all of them.
3. **Contract-first transparency.** A board by bucket (security / functional / quality) with owner, priority and strictness. The platform is transparent; leaders decide.
4. **Definition of done per step plus a triage tree** that routes each failure to an owner. "If it's not terminated, it's not terminated": reflect reality without shame.
5. **Gate ladder:** findable → usable → convenient → secure. Don't discuss convenience before usability.
6. **Sparse sign-off:** each signer answers one cheap question in their own vocabulary. Tier by risk, reuse approved models, start with the ready domains (H22: fear is solved by data).
7. **Work backward from the audience:** "validated" means the last step before execs see it. What will they say? Declare known gaps upfront.
8. **Two phases:** engineers populate the board, directors sign and own it.
9. **Measure against the committed bar**, not the highest. Show percentages. Hide unreliable numbers until they're stable.

## Questions to fire

- "Were *their* cases run, verbatim?"
- "What single question does each signer answer?"
- "Who owns the expectation (the spec)? Product sets the bar, not engineering."
- "What concrete evidence removes this fear?"

## Traps

- More dashboards to answer a trust problem.
- The platform owning go/no-go.
- Reverse-engineering rules from numbers. Rules (the spec) come first; numbers are tunable.
- Us-vs-them on security. Never let one side drive alone.
