# Guide — Cost cuts, budgets and vendor spend

**Load when:** a cost-reduction mandate, a finance review, an annual budget or forecast, a vendor renewal, or an overspend crisis. Cases: F, G, M, Q. Template: `../templates/exec-cost-review.md`.

## Default moves

1. **Order: value → cost → migration** (H21). Explain the tool's value in plain language first ("if we turn it off, users can't approve payments").
2. **Phases: data collection → visibility (showback) → enforcement.** Make phase one precise and leave the rest blurry. Let the exec raise enforcement; don't be the one to propose it.
3. **WHO / HOW / WHEN.** One owner per cost line. Lever scale: green (close the tap) / light orange (low-risk config) / dark orange (redesign) / red (business impact). Pareto batches; put 5 in a batch expecting 3 to land.
4. **One high bar, asymmetric burden, duty plus ability** (H18). Never move the bar. Give owners their breakdown and suggested actions, and turn the fastest team's method into best practice.
5. **Baseline plus ad hoc.** Generic growth for everyone; new projects justify their own extra. Alerts at 80/90/100% of quota.
6. **Swap, don't add; drop the dead weight first.** Money-to-value, not lower spend.
7. **Granularity that implies action.** "Optimize your cost" without a breakdown is chicken-and-egg. The team name tells you who to call; the workload type tells them what to fix.
8. **Forecasts:** re-baseline on reality. Organic regression plus strategic projects by hand. P75, not the worst case. State confidence per number. Don't forecast other teams' growth; give them the past.
9. **Vendor:** tell them the mandate openly; know which lines they earn on and which pass through; ask for specific outputs; use the optimization as an excuse to review designs with domains.

## Questions to fire

- "Who owns this line, by name?"
- "Is this a saving or cost moved between teams?"
- "How confident is each number: 80% or 30%?"
- "Will the exec ask for this breakdown again? Then build it once with traceability."

## Traps

- A uniform percentage cut. A blank-sheet target. A moving bar.
- The platform becomes the driver of other teams' costs ("caretaker, not owner"), so they hand the problem back.
- Throwaway spreadsheets every month instead of a system.
- Over-engineering the visualization. Raw tables plus example queries for smart stakeholders are often enough.

## Team side

Shield the engineers ("if you're scared, I involved you too early"). Visible effort counts: "nobody shoots the police". Claim the savings the system enabled at half-year.
