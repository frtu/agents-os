# Guide — Managing up and new bosses

**Load when:** a new boss or exec joins; misalignment on method with your boss; evaluating a reorg offer; preparing the first 1:1 upward. Case: L.

## Default moves

1. **Ask for the boss's top-down intuition first** ("not a plan, a direction"), then offer the bottom-up view.
2. **A pain-point inventory by who you serve**: how bad, how often, is there a graph? Problems before solutions.
3. **Proposals in order: value → cost → migration** (H21). Steer the boss's idea toward the real value and give him a topic to drive that helps the team.
4. **Proactive sharing.** Otherwise he steers blind and you react for months.
5. **Sync cadence** (for example monthly with the exec) so corrections stay small.
6. **Aligned on the goal, not the method:** prove your method with results. "If they go deep, we listen; if they only care about results, we choose." Don't broadcast the friction.
7. **Good cop / bad cop agreed in advance.** "Leverage me."
8. **Timing:** a teaser now beats perfection in three months, by which time the budgets are gone.
9. **Evaluating a reorg offer:** the person (the relationship), the vision (not "two things taped together"), and growth.

## Questions worth reusing (asked by a good new boss)

- Where are the gaps: mentality, communication or operating model?
- Do proposals come from observed pain or from a video?
- With three more people, would they amplify you or wait to be fed?
- Which customers co-build and which compete?
- What keeps you up at night?

## Traps

- "Everything is perfect."
- Arguing cost before value.
- Describing the predecessor emotionally. Describe them neutrally: strengths and limits.
