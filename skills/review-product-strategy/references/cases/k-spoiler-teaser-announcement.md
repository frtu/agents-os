# Case K — The spoiler announcement

- **Before:** "Join us Tuesday. Speakers: A, B, C. They will discuss X, Y and Z. Agenda: …" — no hook, no reason to attend, and the entire content of the session already read.
- **Common approach:** add emojis and a "see you there!" close — decoration on the same info-dump.
- **Why this one diverges:** an announcement's job is not to inform but to get attention and drive one action (attend, submit a question, accept). It competes for attention once; the structure is hook → takeaway → action, not names → dates → agenda. Spoiling the content removes the only reason to give up a lunch hour. Fake urgency ("seats are limited!" for a townhall) burns credibility for the next announcement.
- **Interrogation:** *"After reading, do they know why to come — but still have a reason to show up?"* (P13). *"Would a busy reader stop scrolling at line 1?"* (P13). *"Who is this for, and what is the one action?"* (P1). *"Is every scarcity claim true — compared to what?"* (P13).
- **What surfaces:** the fix is usually reordering, not adding: hook question first, date/place as a scannable block, audience-participation as the closer ("open Q&A — bring your questions"). Tease the **tension** (the debated question, who's in the room, what's at stake), never the answers. Verified logistics are expectations too: an unknown format suppresses attendance.
- **Distilled (what to do):** hook (the stake) → theme and who's in the room → what the audience controls (Q&A, access) → bold scannable logistics → one action with honest urgency. Word-level polish (hook wording, headers, density) belongs to `rewrite-clarity` (announcement mode, `references/engagement-clarity.md`).
- **Lesson:** sell the seat, not the content.
