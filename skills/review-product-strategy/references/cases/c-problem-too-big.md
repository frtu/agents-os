# Case C — The problem is the whole industry

- **Before:** "Companies spend $400B a year on mental health." / "Developer experience is broken." / "Data quality is a huge problem."
- **Common approach:** add a bigger number or a third-party report to make it feel urgent.
- **Why this one diverges:** a bigger number makes it worse — the gap between problem and product grows. The listener can neither agree nor disagree with "broken", and a payer-less problem has no buyer.
- **Interrogation:** *"Which slice of this do you actually solve?"* *"Whose problem — and who pays? Are they the same person?"* *"Walk me through how it works for them today: steps, time, failure rate."* (P3). *"How do you know — what did they tell you about the last time it happened?"* (P5).
- **What surfaces:** the team has opinions about the pain but no recent, specific stories; payer and user are different people and the deck speaks to neither.
- **Distilled (what to do):** narrow to the slice ("X people need therapy but can't pay for it"). Tell today's workflow concretely ("X manual steps, Y days of delay, Z% approved"). Pick one perspective — the payer, or in a marketplace the side you prioritised. Back it with interview evidence and a workaround that proves the pain.
- **Lesson:** a problem slide is the setup for *your* solution slide. If the solution can't plausibly close it, it's the wrong problem statement.
