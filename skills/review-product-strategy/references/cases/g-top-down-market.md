# Case G — Top-down market

- **Before:** "The global data market is $1T; capturing 1% makes us a $10B company." Internally: "all 200 engineering teams will adopt it."
- **Common approach:** find a bigger or more recent report.
- **Why this one diverges:** top-down numbers aren't specific to your slice and rarely survive one question. The "1% of a huge market" line signals you haven't done the bottoms-up work.
- **Interrogation:** *"How many prospective customers in the segment you serve today?"* *"What is each one worth to you, based on what current customers actually pay or save?"* (P7).
- **What surfaces:** the defensible market is smaller but credible — and often still large enough. When creating a category, an analogy to a large company that did something similar is more convincing than a report.
- **Distilled (what to do):** market = prospective customers × value per customer, from real numbers. Internally: teams that could adopt × hours or cost saved per team, from interviewed teams.
- **Lesson:** a smaller number you can defend beats a big one you can't.
