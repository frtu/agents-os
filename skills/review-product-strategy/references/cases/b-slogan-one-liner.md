# Case B — Slogan instead of a one-liner

- **Before:** "We're reinventing agriculture." / "The intelligent data platform for the enterprise." / "Organizing the world's information."
- **Common approach:** wordsmith the slogan until it sounds bigger.
- **Why this one diverges:** the problem isn't style, it's missing **mechanics**. A slogan only works for people who already know what you do — which is exactly who doesn't need the pitch.
- **Interrogation:** *"After this sentence, what can I picture? Who uses it, doing what?"* (P2). Run three tests: **concrete** ("we build and operate robot greenhouses"), **layperson words** ("help people quit smoking", not "behaviour change among nicotine addicts"), **specific to you** ("plant-based cheese", not "plant-based dairy").
- **What surfaces:** the team often can't agree on the one-liner — a sign the narrative itself is unsettled.
- **Distilled (what to do):** describe what happens: "You type what you want to know into a box; we return an ordered list of pages ranked by how many others link to them." Then chain it to a traction teaser.
- **Lesson:** describe the mechanism, not the ambition. Marketing words ("platform", "first ever", "unique") are fluff the listener must filter out.
