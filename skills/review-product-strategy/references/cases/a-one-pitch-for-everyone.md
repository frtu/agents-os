# Case A — One pitch for everyone

- **Before:** the product deck used in sales calls is reused for the funding or exec review. It opens with industry acronyms, a feature tour and a live demo; the website hero text and the deck's first slide are identical.
- **Common approach:** "trim the deck" — remove a few slides, keep the content.
- **Why this one diverges:** trimming keeps the wrong *frame*. A customer asks "does it fix *my* problem?"; an investor or exec, who may never have had the problem, asks "can this become big, and can this team build it?". Same facts, different argument.
- **Interrogation:** *"Who is in the room, and do they have this problem?"* (P1). *"Would a layperson picture what you do from slide 1?"* (P2). *"Which slide answers 'how big can this get'?"*
- **What surfaces:** the investor-type questions — stage, market, how you charge, what you know that others don't, why this team — have no slide. The jargon that builds credibility with customers reads as noise here.
- **Distilled (what to do):** keep two pitches. Customer pitch: their jargon, their workflow, demo, onboarding, their price; the customer talks more than you. Investor / exec pitch: plain words, mechanics not slogans, the six investor questions, numbers over screenshots. If the website and the deck say the same thing (and the buyer isn't the user), one of them is wrong.
- **Lesson:** different motivations, different pitches. Founders typically take weeks to accept it.
