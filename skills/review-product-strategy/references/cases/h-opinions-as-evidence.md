# Case H — Opinions as evidence

- **Before:** "90% of respondents said they would use the feature." "Everyone we talked to loved the demo." "Teams clearly need this."
- **Common approach:** run a bigger survey.
- **Why this one diverges:** future-tense answers and compliments are cheap — people are polite, especially after a demo. More of a weak signal is still weak.
- **Interrogation:** *"Tell me about the last time one of them hit this problem. What did it cost?"* *"What do they do today instead? Did they build or buy a workaround?"* *"Did anyone commit to anything — time, a pilot, budget, an intro?"* (P5). *"Did you show the solution before asking about the problem?"*
- **What surfaces:** few specific stories, no cost data, no workaround — or, positively, a scrappy spreadsheet or script a team built, which is the strongest pain evidence available.
- **Distilled (what to do):** re-run discovery with past-tense questions, no pitching until the close, dig for frequency × time × cost, capture verbatim quotes with permission, end by asking for a commitment. Log each pain point with its source. Mine existing signals too: tickets, postmortems, escalations, churn notes.
- **Lesson:** past behaviour and commitments are evidence; opinions about the future are not.
