# Case J — Slide overload

- **Before:** 30 slides, 8 bullets each, an architecture diagram, three screenshots; the meeting ends without questions.
- **Common approach:** shrink the font, speed up the talk.
- **Why this one diverges:** silence isn't agreement — it means the deck was too complex to be curious about. Every added piece of information costs decision time (Hick's law).
- **Interrogation:** *"Flash this slide to someone new — can they say its point immediately?"* (P10). *"Which 5–7 ideas must they remember?"* *"Is this a 15–20 minute overview that sets up a conversation, or a download?"* (P12).
- **What surfaces:** most slides are topic titles ("Architecture", "Metrics") instead of claims; the narrative spine was never written.
- **Distilled (what to do):** write the 10–15 claim spine first; one claim = one slide; the title *is* the claim. Large bold type, high contrast, caption the conclusion on charts; replace screenshots with a bulleted list of steps. Everything else → appendix. Exception: overwhelm on purpose only when the point is the complexity of the problem.
- **Lesson:** legible, simple, obvious. Audiences invest in people, not slides.
