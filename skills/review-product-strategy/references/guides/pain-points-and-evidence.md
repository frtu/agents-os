# Guide — Pain points and evidence

**Load when:** the strategy or pitch rests on a customer problem and you need to judge — or help gather — the evidence: interviews, pain points, testimonials, workarounds. Cases: C, H. Templates: `../templates/pain-point-evidence-log.md`, `../templates/customer-interview-guide.md`.

## Default moves

1. **Define the slice and the payer.** Which part of the problem, for whom, and who pays (payer ≠ user in healthcare, HR, platforms).
2. **Interview about the past.** "Tell me about the last time…" — steps, frequency, time, cost, who else was involved. Don't pitch before the close.
3. **Find the workaround.** A spreadsheet, script or tool they built or bought is the strongest pain proof; no workaround may mean low pain.
4. **Mine existing signals.** Support tickets, incident postmortems, escalations, churn / cancellation notes, retro boards, sales-call notes.
5. **Log every pain point with its source** and capture verbatim quotes with permission — they become testimonials.
6. **Ask for a commitment** at the end (intro, pilot, time, budget). Commitment beats compliments.
7. **Quantify into a before / after** that the solution slide mirrors.

## Evidence strength

| Strong | Weak |
|--------|------|
| Specific recent story with numbers | "That sounds useful" |
| Existing workaround or spend | "I would definitely use it" |
| Commitment: pilot, intro, budget, time | Compliments after a demo |
| Payer confirms they'd pay / fund | Only users interviewed |
| Recurring in tickets / postmortems | Founder or team conviction |

## Questions to fire

- "How many interviews, with whom (payer or user), and when?"
- "What's the most recent story, and what did it cost them?"
- "What do they do today instead?"
- "Who committed to anything?"

## Traps

- Surveys of hypotheticals presented as demand (Case H).
- Pitching during discovery — turns it into polite feedback.
- One loud customer generalised to a market.
- Testimonials without permission or without a role attached.

## Signals of success

The problem slide uses the customer's own words and numbers; a stranger can repeat the pain in one sentence; at least one interviewee asks when they can have it.
