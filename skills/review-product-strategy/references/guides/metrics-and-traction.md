# Guide — Metrics, traction and market size

**Load when:** the deck's traction, unit economics or market slides are in question. Cases: E, G. Pairs with `product-strategy-critique-methodology.md` → *Unit Economics & Business Model Rigor*.

## Default moves

1. **Lead with the metric this audience values most** — revenue for investors (plus how it's earned), adoption / toil saved / cost avoided for an internal exec — and explain its driver.
2. **Tell the standard story:** growing fast on a solid model → acquiring customers profitably (CAC, payback, organic vs paid; sales cycle, ACV for enterprise) → customers love it (retention, engagement, adoption within org).
3. **Pick 2–4 hero facts** — the numbers you want repeated to partners.
4. **Trends over snapshots:** per-month or per-quarter, ≥ 4–6 periods; time-to-milestone shows speed. The longer you've existed, the more you must show.
5. **Define every number** (monthly vs daily actives, gross vs net).
6. **Market bottoms-up:** prospective customers in served segments × real value per customer. Analogies for new categories.

## Questions to fire

- "Which 2–4 numbers should they remember?"
- "Why is this cumulative? Show per period."
- "What exactly does 'user' / 'revenue' mean here?"
- "Where does the market number come from — your customers or a report?"
- "Can you rattle off burn, runway, CAC, LTV without notes?"

## Traps

- Too many numbers / too few / unclear (cumulative, double axes, undefined).
- TAM presented as SAM; "1% of a huge market".
- Hockey-stick projections with no stated assumptions — financials are about logic, not accuracy.
