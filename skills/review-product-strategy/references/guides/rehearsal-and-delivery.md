# Guide — Slide clarity, rehearsal and delivery

**Load when:** the deck is substantively right but doesn't land — too dense, no questions asked, presenter unprepared, or the user is about to pitch; also announcements and invites. The matching cases are listed in `../cases/README.md` (cases point here through their `guide` field). Delegate word-level fixes to `rewrite-clarity` (its presentation and announcement themes).

## Default moves

1. **Legible, simple, obvious.** Large bold type, contrast, key text at top; one idea per slide; caption the conclusion. 5–7 ideas to be remembered.
2. **Kill distractions:** screenshots (use a 3-step list), diagram mazes, animations, memes, excessive branding, uncaptioned photos.
3. **Flash test** each slide on someone new.
4. **Practice hour, 20 / 20 / 20:** timed pitch → conversation → feedback. Ask: *Confused when? Bored when? Did the deck help or hurt? Which hard questions need great answers?*
5. **Video pitch:** one presenter, camera at eye level, lit face, quiet room, bullet script not read aloud; record and watch yourself.
6. **Refine daily:** 1–2 h after each meeting — where they leaned in, where they zoned out, which objections had weak answers, which spine claim is weakest. Update deck and Q&A bank.
7. **Prepare for diligence:** they will look you up and compare claims to public data. Fix discrepancies first.

## Questions to fire

- "How long is the pitch, timed?"
- "Did your last audience ask questions? Which ones?"
- "Which objection did you answer worst?"

## Traps

- Reading the script; switching presenters mid-pitch.
- Treating the deck as a script — it's a conversation starter.
- Rushing — pause after the strongest points; know the numbers cold.

## Announcements & invites

Same landing discipline, different artifact: an announcement gets one glance, not 20 minutes.

1. **Hook → takeaway → action.** Hook question or stake in line 1–2; date/place/time as a bold scannable block; end with the one action and why now.
2. **Teaser sells the seat, not the content.** Name the tension and who's in the room; never the answers. The audience's part (open Q&A, meet the speakers) is always a safe, true hook.
3. **Honest urgency only.** "Seats limited", "tomorrow", "questions in advance" must all be literally true.
4. **Format is an expectation.** Unknown format (how long, Q&A or not, mingle?) suppresses attendance — state it.
