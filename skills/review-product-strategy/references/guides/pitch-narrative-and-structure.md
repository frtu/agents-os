# Guide — Pitch narrative and structure

**Load when:** reviewing or preparing a pitch deck, product presentation, funding ask or internal initiative pitch, and the issue is *what the story is* or *in which order*. Cases: A, B, D, F, I. Template: `../templates/pitch-deck.md`.

## Default moves

1. **Name the audience first.** Investor / exec (can this be big? can this team build it?) or customer / adopting team (does it fix my problem?). Two audiences, two pitches (P1).
2. **Write the spine before slides.** 10–15 claims, evidence under each; one claim = one slide; the title is the claim. A claim without evidence goes back to discovery.
3. **Order: attention → trust → conviction → action.** One-liner + traction teaser → problem → solution → in-depth traction → insight / moat → market → competition → team → vision → ask → appendix.
4. **Credibility before vision.** What is built and used comes first; the vision must extend it (P8).
5. **End on the ask.** Amount, use, milestone; show the obstacle is resources (P11).
6. **Overview, not download.** 15–20 minutes to set up a 40–45 minute conversation; prepared questions go to the appendix (P12).

## Investor's six questions (Seibel)

What you do (simplest words) · how far along · market size · how you charge · what you know that others don't · who the founders are and can they build it. Plus **why now?**

## Stage calibration

| | Seed-style | Series-A-style |
|---|---|---|
| Built on | Team, insight, early signal | What's been built: trends, unit economics |
| Deck | ~10 slides, ≤ 3 per topic | 10–15 + appendix |
| Ask buys | Being A-ready in ~12 months | Next 18–24 months of traction |

## Internal initiative mapping

Exec sponsor = investor; adopting teams = customers; traction = adoption, toil saved, incidents avoided; business model = cost / funding model; market = teams × value per team; competition = status quo, buy vs build, another team's tool; use of funds = headcount / budget / mandate + the milestone it buys.

## Questions to fire

- "Who decides, and what decision do you want at the end?"
- "Which slide answers each of the six investor questions?"
- "What have you already built that makes the vision believable?"
- "Where is the ask, and what milestone does it buy?"

## Traps

- Merging the customer and investor pitch (Case A).
- A deck that is the roadmap (Case F).
- Thiel-style combined problem/solution slide used to skip the evidence.
- "No competition" or a feature checkbox grid instead of honest positioning, including the status quo.
